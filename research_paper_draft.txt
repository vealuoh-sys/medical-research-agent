**Title:** Diagnostic Accuracy of Point-of-Care HbA1c Testing: A Systematic Review

**Abstract:**

**Background:** Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) is a crucial biomarker for assessing long-term glycemic control in diabetic patients. Point-of-care (POC) HbA1c testing has gained popularity due to its convenience and potential for improved patient outcomes.

**Methods:** A systematic search of multiple databases was conducted to identify studies evaluating the diagnostic accuracy of POC HbA1c testing. A total of 62 records were screened, and 36 studies were included in the review.

**Results:** Due to insufficient data reporting across included studies, quantitative meta-analytic pooling was not possible. The results of individual studies are presented, highlighting the variability in diagnostic accuracy and the need for standardized reporting.

**Conclusion:** This systematic review highlights the limitations of current evidence on POC HbA1c testing due to incomplete data reporting. Further studies with complete 2x2 data are necessary to enable quantitative meta-analytic pooling and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the diagnostic accuracy of POC HbA1c testing.

**Introduction:**

The management of diabetes mellitus relies heavily on accurate monitoring of glycemic control. Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) is a widely accepted biomarker for assessing long-term glycemic control. Point-of-care (POC) HbA1c testing has emerged as a convenient and potentially effective tool for improving patient outcomes. However, the diagnostic accuracy of POC HbA1c testing is crucial for its effective implementation in clinical practice. This systematic review aims to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of POC HbA1c testing.

**Methods:**

A systematic search of multiple databases was conducted using a predefined search strategy. The search yielded 62 records, which were screened for eligibility. A total of 36 studies were included in the review. Data extraction was performed using a standardized form, and the quality of included studies was assessed using the QUADAS-2 tool.

**Results:**

The PRISMA flow diagram is presented below:

* Total records screened: 62
* Included studies: 36
* Excluded studies: 20
* Flagged for review: 6

Due to insufficient data reporting across included studies, quantitative meta-analytic pooling was not possible. The results of individual studies are presented in the extraction table (extraction_table.csv).

**Discussion and Limitations:**

The findings of this systematic review highlight the limitations of current evidence on POC HbA1c testing due to incomplete data reporting. The lack of standardized reporting and the variability in diagnostic accuracy across studies underscore the need for further research with complete 2x2 data. The clinical utility of POC HbA1c testing depends on its diagnostic accuracy, and therefore, it is essential to address these limitations to provide a more comprehensive understanding of its effectiveness.
